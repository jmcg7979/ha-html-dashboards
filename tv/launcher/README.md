# TV launcher

A small Android app for Google TV that shows a `tv` dashboard full screen as the TV's home screen, and gives the page what a web page can't do on its own: the installed apps and launching them, Continue watching, sign-in to Home Assistant, and take-overs (the timer alarm, camera alerts) shown over a playing app. Java with no libraries; built without Gradle.

It is a reference, shared as-is like the rest of this repo. It was built and tested on Google TV; other Android TV devices may differ.

## What it does
- **Home screen:** `HomeActivity` is a full-screen WebView on your dashboard URL (e.g. `http://<ha>:8123/local/dash/start.html?d=tv&p=tv`). The page reaches the app through `window.tvhost` (`engine/core/tvhost.js`): installed apps, launching one, opening a link, Android Settings. App banners are served to the page at `<ha origin>/__tv/banner/<package>`. Back and Home reach the page as `tv:back` / `tv:home`; on a page of your server that isn't the dashboard (HA's sign-in page) Back goes back and Home loads the dashboard.
- **Sign-in:** the app answers HA's app-auth protocol (`window.externalApp.getExternalAuth`) with a long-lived token you give it, so the dashboard signs in with no login screen. A token belongs to the server it was sent for: only a page on that server gets it, and pointing the app at another address gives that server no token.
- **What a page may do:** the two bridges answer only a page on the configured dashboard's server (or the built-in page). The top page stays on that server: a link or a redirect to another one is not followed. A link from the page starts only these: the Play Store page of an app (`market://details?id=<package>`), an installed app (`dashtv://launch/<package>`), Android Settings (`intent:#Intent;action=android.settings.SETTINGS;end`), a web address in one named app (`intent://<address>#Intent;scheme=https;package=<package>;end`), and a Continue watching item's own link, word for word as its app published it (`Links.java`). Android can't tell the app which frame of a page is calling, so the engine shows no `frame:` view inside the launcher.
- **Versions:** `tvhost.caps()` answers `{ api, version, overlay, can: [method names] }`; the engine reads it once (`core/tvhost.js`: `api`, `can()`), and calls only what the installed launcher has, so a newer dashboard runs on an older launcher and the other way round. The build's name is also in the user agent (`DashTV/<version>`). `api` 2: the take-over window knows `aside`.
- **Continue watching:** many apps publish what you're in the middle of to Android TV's Watch Next list (Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max, Hulu, Emby in our tests; not YouTube). The `watchnext` card shows it, and OK opens that episode.
- **Home button:** Google TV won't let another app be the real Home. **Never disable the stock launcher** (`com.google.android.apps.tv.launcherx`): the TV then falls back to an empty system Home that restarts every minute or two and launches nothing (fix: re-enable "Google TV" in Settings > Apps > System apps). Instead, `HomeGuard`, an accessibility service, opens this app whenever the stock home screen comes to the front. It sees only the window changes of the stock launcher and of the cast screen, and reads nothing on screen (`res/xml/home_guard.xml`). Switch it off or uninstall the app, and the stock home screen is back.
- **Take-overs over a playing app:** while another app is in front, HomeGuard keeps the dashboard loaded at `&overlay=1` (only the take-overs) in a WebView with no window. When the page shows a take-over, its window goes up over the app and takes the remote: Back or OK stops the timer or dismisses the alert; the window comes down when the page says it is done, and also when that page starts over. A camera alert shown as a small picture in its corner (`alerts.cameras.corner`, `engine/core/alerts.js`) is `aside`: its window takes neither the remote nor touches, so the app behind is used as before, and only Back is taken, once, to dismiss the picture. For that HomeGuard asks Android for the remote's keys (`canRequestFilterKeyEvents`) only while such a picture is up. As a backup, `Watcher` follows the trigger entities the page names (`tvhost.watch`) over one HA WebSocket with the same token: a state that starts with no page loaded loads one, and a change made while its link was down for up to five minutes still counts.
- **Voice:** `tvhost.listen()` starts Android's speech recognizer (`Ear.java`; on Google TV, Google's), and what it hears reaches the page as `tv:hear` events: `listening`, `words` (so far), `done` (the final words), or `error` (`nomatch`, `silence`, `mic`, `network`, `busy`, `other`). `stopListening()` ends it; the screen leaving the front ends it too. `canListen()` is false without the microphone permission or a recognizer, and the page then shows no mic. The remote's own mic button is no use for this: Google TV sends it straight to Assistant, no app ever sees it.
- **Music Assistant:** when MA plays to the TV, its own cast screen comes to the front. While the page asks for it (`tvhost.cover`), HomeGuard brings this app back over it on the page's Now playing; the music keeps playing. Behind the screensaver or another app the page is stopped and can't ask, so the page also gives the launcher the TV's MA player to follow (`wake` on the Now playing bar, `tvhost.watch`): when it starts playing its own queue (`has: 'active_queue'` on the row: MA's player mirrors the TV's Cast and also says "playing" for a YouTube video, without a queue), this app comes to the front on Now playing, and the page, running again, asks to cover. A row may also name the player app that plays that music on the TV (`play: '<package>'`, the player's `maApp`): the launcher then sends the app a Play media key as the music starts. A player app that lost the sound to another app for good (a video watched in YouTube) plays muted when its server starts it again, and only a Play asked on the TV itself makes it take the sound back (SendSpin Player 2.0.0, by design: `adb logcat -s AudioFocus` says "not taking the output: another app owns it", then "focus requested: granted" after the key); `adb logcat -s DashTV` has "watcher: Play to <package>".
- **Opening an app from outside:** the link `dashtv://launch/<package>` opens that app (`Launch`), e.g. as the `activity` of Home Assistant's `remote.turn_on` (Android TV Remote). HA's own way with a bare package name asks the Play Store (`market://launch?id=`), which Google TV stopped answering in November 2026.
- **Screensaver:** `Saver` shows the house's photos, one every 40 s with a 2.5 s fade, and over them, bottom left (moving a little each minute against burn-in), the time, the date and the weather now. Photos: HA's `sensor.screensaver_photos` lists them (attribute `photos`, file names; `sites/example/ha-config/packages/screensaver_photos.yaml` makes the sensor and says how to fill it), each is loaded from `/media/local/screensaver/<file>` with the TV's token (HA's media folder: not served without one), shuffled, none twice until all were shown, the list read again each round. A photo the screen's shape fills it; any other is shown whole over a blurred, dimmed copy of itself (a 64 px copy blurred in software, drawn full screen). Photos are kept as hardware bitmaps (graphics memory only) and a slide that has faded out frees its photo at once. The weather line: the page names the entity (`tvhost.saver({ weather, conditions })`, sent by the status line; kept as `saver` in the settings), `Lines.java` words it. No token, no address or no photos: the clock on black, in a new place every minute, as before. Plain views in a process of its own (`:saver`, about 40 MB with photos, 25 MB with the clock only); nothing moves but the fade. Choose it with `adb shell settings put secure screensaver_components dash.tv/dash.tv.Saver`; the TV's own one is back with its component name there (Google TV: `com.google.android.apps.tv.dreamx/.service.Backdrop`).
- **No screensaver while sound plays:** Google TV only counts presses of the remote, so music from a player app that shows nothing, from a phone, or behind this screen ran into the screensaver and then into sleep, still playing. While a media player on the TV is running and sound comes out, the app holds a screen wake lock (`Awake`; no permission needed: Android tells any app which players run, not whose they are). It keeps an awake TV awake whatever is in front; it wakes nothing and ends no screensaver that is already up. The screensaver's wait starts again when the sound stops. `--ez awake false` turns it off.
- **The Cast receiver held** (`--ez cast true`, off by default): the part of Google's Cast receiver that listens for casts runs as a plain started service, which Android counts among the cached processes and ends first when memory runs out; on a 2 GB TV it came back deaf. With the switch on the app binds that service (`CastHold`), and since a process counts as much as whoever is bound to it, the receiver then ranks with this app (visible, while HomeGuard is on). Check: `adb shell dumpsys activity processes | grep 'Proc #.*mediashell'` says `vis` where it said `cch`.
- **Wireless debugging kept on** (`--ez adb true`, off by default; needs the grant in step 3): Android switches Wireless debugging off at every restart and when the Wi-Fi drops; the app switches it on again (`AdbKeep`), so the computer you paired still gets in, on the port the TV advertises (`adb mdns services`). Android only lets it on by itself on a Wi-Fi access point where "Always allow on this network" was chosen once, and that is per access point, not per network name: on another one the TV asks on its screen. The app tries at most twice per Wi-Fi connection, so a TV nobody answers asks twice and is then left alone until its Wi-Fi connects again or it restarts.
- **Offline:** when HA doesn't answer, `assets/fallback.html` shows every installed app, Settings and Try again, and the app tries HA again every 20 s. The same page shows when HA answers but the dashboard doesn't come to run within 45 s (a broken deploy; the engine says it runs by setting `window.DASH_UP`); the dashboard is then tried again after five minutes.
- **When Android takes the page:** on a TV short of memory Android may end the WebView's renderer (the process that runs the page), and a page can crash it. The app stays up either way: the page is dropped, and loaded again at once when the home screen is in front, otherwise the next time it is shown. A dashboard that dies three times within a minute is not loaded a fourth time: the built-in page shows, and the dashboard is tried again after five minutes or with Try again. One line per event in `adb logcat -s DashTV`. With `--ez yield true` a page that is out of sight also lets Android take it before other apps' services (the Cast receiver) when memory runs out; the price is a page load the next time it is needed (a couple of seconds for Home, and for a take-over that starts meanwhile).

## Build
`tv/launcher/build.sh --new-key [out.apk]` the first time, `tv/launcher/build.sh [out.apk]` after that, on Linux. It needs:
- JDK 17: `JAVA_HOME`, default `~/.local/opt/jdk17`
- Android SDK command-line tools with `platforms;android-34` and `build-tools;34.0.0`: `ANDROID_HOME`, default `~/.local/opt/android-sdk`
- `adb` (Android SDK platform-tools) to install it

`--new-key` makes a signing key in `TV_KEY_DIR` (default `~/.config/dash-tv`) if there is none. **Keep it and back it up:** an APK signed with another key can't update the installed app, which is why a build without the key stops instead of making one.

Checks, neither needs a TV: `tv/launcher/build.sh check` compiles against the Android library alone with every javac warning on; `tv/launcher/test.sh` runs the tests of the plain-Java parts (whose page is this, which links may start, what starts a take-over) on the JDK. `tv/launcher/build.sh version` prints the build a checkout would make; a TV's own is `adb shell dumpsys package dash.tv | grep version`.

## Set up a TV
Run these from the machine that built the APK, on the same network as the TV.
1. On the TV: Settings > System > About > Android TV OS build: press OK 7 times to unlock Developer options. Then Settings > System > Developer options > Wireless debugging on > Pair device with pairing code.
2. Pair and connect (IP, port and code from that screen; the connect port is the one on the Wireless debugging screen itself):
   ```
   adb pair <tv ip>:<pair port> <code>
   adb connect <tv ip>:<port>
   ```
3. Install it and give it what it needs:
   ```
   adb install -r ~/.cache/dash-tv/dash-tv.apk
   adb shell pm grant dash.tv android.permission.READ_TV_LISTINGS
   adb shell appops set dash.tv TURN_SCREEN_ON allow
   adb shell pm grant dash.tv android.permission.WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS
   adb shell pm grant dash.tv android.permission.RECORD_AUDIO
   adb shell "am broadcast -a dash.tv.PROVISION -n dash.tv/.Provision --es url 'http://<ha>:8123/local/dash/start.html?d=tv&p=tv' --es token '<token>'"
   adb shell settings put secure enabled_accessibility_services dash.tv/dash.tv.HomeGuard
   adb shell settings put secure accessibility_enabled 1
   adb shell am start -n dash.tv/.HomeActivity
   ```
   The `settings put` line replaces the list of accessibility services that are on. If `adb shell settings get secure enabled_accessibility_services` shows others, add HomeGuard to them with `:` between.
   `READ_TV_LISTINGS` is for Continue watching; `TURN_SCREEN_ON` lets music that starts on the TV wake it to Now playing; `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` is only for keeping Wireless debugging on (`--ez adb true`): leave it out if you don't want that. `RECORD_AUDIO` is for voice in the page (`tvhost.listen`, Ear.java): the TV's speech recognizer hears only while the page asks (a mic button pressed), never on its own; leave it out and the page shows no mic.
4. Press Home: your dashboard comes up.

**Settings** (`Provision`, from adb only: the receiver needs the DUMP permission, which apps can't get): `--es url`, `--es token` (for the server of the `url` sent with it, or of the one already set; each server keeps its own), `--es id` / `--es name` (free text the page can read through `tvhost.info()`), `--ez guard false` (stock home screen back), `--ez overlay false` (no take-overs over apps), `--ez yield true` (above), `--ez awake false`, `--ez cast true`, `--ez adb true` (all three above), `--ez debug true` (the pages can be inspected over adb with Chrome's DevTools protocol), `--ez photos false` (the screensaver shows no photos, only the clock and the weather), `--ez probe true` (changes nothing: the answer is the TV's Watch Next list), `--ez clear true` (forget everything). Extras you leave out keep their value.

**Sign-in:** make an HA user for the TVs (not an admin, local access only), sign in as that user, and create a long-lived token in its profile. The token is kept in the app's private storage. Without one, the page shows HA's login screen.

**Undo:** remove HomeGuard from `enabled_accessibility_services` (or `--ez guard false`), then `adb uninstall dash.tv`.

## Good to know
- **Don't `am force-stop dash.tv`.** Google TV then switches off the app's accessibility service, so HomeGuard and the take-overs stay off until you switch it on again (step 3).
- Memory: a WebView that sits in a window keeps tens of MB of graphics memory, shown or not. Behind another app the home screen's WebView leaves its window and the take-over page has none until it shows something; the pages stay loaded.
- If the app itself crashes twice in a row, Android leaves its accessibility service off for half an hour, then an hour; Google's home screen is up meanwhile. Installing again (or switching the service off and on) brings it back.
- Wireless debugging and adb on a fixed port don't survive a reboot. The launcher itself does: HomeGuard brings it up at start-up, and with `--ez adb true` it switches Wireless debugging on again (the fixed port stays gone).
- The app allows plain `http` to reach HA on your LAN (`usesCleartextTraffic`). Use `https` in the URL if your HA has it.
- The TV has the token's rights: anything that user can see or control in HA, someone with the remote can too.

## Looking inside a TV's page
With `--ez debug true` the WebViews can be inspected over adb (Chrome's DevTools protocol on the socket `webview_devtools_remote_<pid>`: `adb forward tcp:9333 localabstract:webview_devtools_remote_$(adb shell pidof dash.tv)`, then `chrome://inspect` or any CDP client on port 9333). That is how to see what a TV page costs: its memory, its GPU layers, and whether it keeps drawing frames while nothing happens. A 2 GB Google TV has little of either to spare (`engine/docs/design-system.md`, Screens).

## The page side
A TV page is a normal dashboard with `profile: 'tv'` (960×540 CSS px on every Google TV, no scrolling) and `remote: true` (`engine/core/remote.js`: arrows move the highlight, OK taps, Back closes a pop-up). Cards made for it: `statusbar`, `apps`, `watchnext`. `sites/example/dash/dashboards/tv.js` is a starting point. Try it in a browser on the mock: `http://localhost:8125/local/dash/start.html?d=tv&p=tv&tvdemo=1` (`tvdemo` fills Continue watching with made-up shows; the mock draws stand-in app banners). Check that every page fits:
```
SHOT_SCRIPT=fitcheck.mjs engine/_dev/tools/shot.sh mock <site> -- --strict --d tv --q p=tv --sizes 960x540 --out /tmp/fit
```
